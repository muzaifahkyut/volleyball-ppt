Welcome to Volleyball

A sport of strength, agility, and teamwork.

Introduction to Volleyball

Volleyball is a team sport played by two teams of six players each, separated by a net. The goal is to hit a ball over the net such that it lands in the opponent's court without being returned properly. It requires a mix of strength, agility, coordination, and teamwork, and is played both indoors and outdoors (beach volleyball, with 2 players per team, is a popular variant).

History of Volleyball

  • Volleyball was invented in 1895 by William G. Morgan, a physical education director in Holyoke, Massachusetts, USA. He wanted a game that was less physically intense than basketball but still required teamwork.
  • Originally called "mintonette," it was renamed "volleyball" in 1896 because players "volley" the ball back and forth over the net.

Rules of Playing Volleyball

Number of Players

Volleyball is played by two teams, with six players on each team on the court at one time. The players are arranged into front-row and back-row positions, and each player has a specific role in offense and defense.

Scoring System

Volleyball uses the rally scoring system, meaning a point is scored on every rally, no matter which team served the ball. A set is won by the team that reaches 25 points with a 2-point lead. Matches are usually played in a best-of-five sets format.

Serving the Ball

The serve starts the game and each rally. The server must stand behind the end line and hit the ball over the net into the opponent’s court. The serve must go over the net without touching the ground first. If the serve goes out or into the net, the opposing team earns the point.

Three-Hit Rule

Each team is allowed only three hits to return the ball over the net. These hits are usually a pass, set, and spike. Blocking at the net does not count as one of the three hits.

Rotation Rule

When a team wins the right to serve, all players must rotate clockwise to their next position. This rule ensures that every player takes turns serving and playing in both front-row and back-row positions.

Double Contact Rule

A player is not allowed to hit the ball twice in a row. This prevents one player from controlling the ball continuously. An exception is during a block, where the blocker may contact the ball again immediately.

Ball In and Out

The ball is considered in if it touches any part of the court lines. It is considered out if it lands completely outside the lines, touches an antenna, or hits an object outside the court.

Net and Line Violations

Players are not allowed to touch the net during play or cross the center line if it interferes with the opponent. During a serve, the server must not step on or cross the service line until the ball is hit.

Player Roles

Setter

Sets the ball for attackers and directs the team’s offense.

Outside Hitter (Left-side Hitter)

Main attacker; plays both offense and defense.

Opposite Hitter (Right-side Hitter)

Attacks from the right side and helps block the opponent’s outside hitter.

Middle Blocker (Middle Hitter)

Focuses on blocking at the net and quick attacks in the middle.

Libero

A defensive specialist who wears a different jersey and plays in the back row; cannot serve, block, or attack above the net.

Defensive Specialist

Replaces front-row players in the back row to improve defense.

Conclusion

Volleyball combines strategy, skill, and teamwork, making it an exciting sport for players and fans alike. It's been an Olympic sport since 1964 and continues to grow worldwide. Whether indoors or on the beach, give it a try!

Fun fact: The first volleyball game was played with a basketball bladder!
